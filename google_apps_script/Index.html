Key takeaways

  1. The +4.3% topline growth is not coming from the main channels.

    Google, Facebook and Email together are down 6.4% ($4.75M to $4.45M). All of the growth, and more, comes from "Other channels," which jumped $536K (+75%). That one bucket is the reason the topline looks healthy.

  2. Facebook's drop and the "Other" jump look like mirror images.

    Facebook lost 9.5 points of share while "Other" gained 8.9 points. When one channel loses about the same share another gains, it often means revenue is being credited to a different channel, not actually lost. Common causes include broken or missing UTM tags on FB ads, landing URL changes, or FB traffic falling into Direct or Unassigned. Check this before concluding that Facebook performance fell 17%.

  3. C100 and GA disagree on direction.

    C100 is down 6.7% while GA Total is up 4.3%. If C100 is the backend or source-of-truth figure, this gap is a red flag: GA may be overcounting this month, possibly through duplicate purchase events or the same "Other" spike. C100 was about 10.3% of GA revenue in September and about 9.2% in October, so the two are drifting apart.

  4. The real wins are Email and Google.

    Email is up 21.8%. It's the smallest channel but the fastest growing, so it's worth identifying which campaigns drove it. Google is growing steadily at +3.4%.

Summary table

Month to date, day 1 to 19 of each month. Other channels = GA Total minus Google, Facebook and Email.

Recommended checks before sharing widely

  1. Break "Other channels" down into Direct, Organic, Referral, Unassigned and so on, to find which one grew.
  2. Audit FB campaign UTMs and landing URLs for any changes made in late September or October.
  3. Reconcile C100 against GA transactions, comparing order counts as well as revenue, to rule out duplicate purchase events.
